🏭 What is a Pneumatic Conveying System?

A pneumatic conveying system uses pressurized air or a vacuum to move dry bulk materials — powders, granules, pellets — through enclosed pipelines. It is efficient, dust-free, saves floor space, and protects products from contamination compared to mechanical belt conveyors.

🔩 Major Components
💨
Air Mover (Blower / Compressor)
The engine of the system. Generates the airflow needed to push or pull material through the pipeline.
🔄
Rotary Airlock Valve
Feeds material safely into the airflow while keeping the system sealed to prevent pressure leaks.
🔧
Conveying Pipeline
Stainless or carbon steel pipe through which the material travels from source to destination.
🌀
Filter Receiver / Cyclone
At the destination, separates solid material from the carrier air and filters out fine dust.
🏗️
Storage Silo / Receiver
The final vessel where bulk material is collected after transport.
⚙️ How It Works — 4 Steps
1
FEEDING
Dry bulk material is fed into the pipeline in a controlled, measured way through the feeding device.
2
CONVEYING
The blower pushes (positive pressure) or pulls (vacuum) the material suspended in the airstream through the pipe.
3
SEPARATING
At the receiving end, a filter receiver or cyclone separator stops the material and drops it into the silo.
4
EXHAUSTING
The filtered, clean air is safely exhausted to atmosphere or recycled back into the system.

🔴 Pressure Trend
Tracks system pressure drop in real-time over a 60-second window. Sudden spikes indicate blockage events or filter clogging. A steady rising trend suggests increasing material loading.
🌬️ Airflow Trend
Monitors volumetric airflow (m³/s) through the blower. Drops in airflow signal air leaks, blower degradation, or filter restriction. A stable flat line means healthy blower operation.
🌡️ Temperature Trend
Conveying temperature affects air density and particle behavior. High temperatures reduce air density, increasing velocity for the same flow rate. Useful for thermal-sensitive materials like polymers and food powders.
⚡ Power Trend
Live blower power consumption in kW. Rising power with constant flow suggests increasing system resistance (filter clog, partial blockage). Combined with airflow trend, this is the primary blower health indicator.

📋 WHY DO WE MONITOR THESE? — Safety Guide
Plain-language explanation of each safety concern and what can go wrong
🔴
High Pressure Drop
Air inside the pipe is under excessive pressure. If it rises too high, the pipe can rupture or the motor can trip — just like a blocked water hose building up pressure.
✓ Safe: <50 kPa
△ Warn: 50–80 kPa
⚠ Critical: >80 kPa
🟡
Pipe Blockage
If air velocity drops too low, powder or material settles and blocks the pipe — just like slow water allowing sediment to build up in a drain. The entire system can shut down.
✓ Safe: v/v_salt >0.5
△ Warn: 0.25–0.45
⚠ Critical: <0.25
⚡
Blower Overload
The blower motor is working beyond its rated capacity. Like a car engine overheating on a steep hill at full load — the motor can burn out or trip the factory power supply.
✓ Safe: <70% load
△ Warn: 70–90%
⚠ Critical: >90%
🌀
Filter Clogging
The bag filter's job is to capture dust and release clean air. If it clogs, air pressure rises and the system slows or stops — just like a dirty AC filter reducing airflow.
✓ Safe: <500 Pa
△ Warn: 500–1200 Pa
⚠ Critical: >1200 Pa
🔧
Pipe Erosion
When material travels at high speed through pipe bends, it slowly wears away the pipe wall — like sand-laden water eroding rock over time. Ultimately the pipe can fail and burst.
✓ Safe: <40% risk
△ Warn: 40–70%
⚠ Critical: >70%
💥
Dust Explosion Risk ⚠ MOST DANGEROUS
Fine dust from flour, cement, or coal mixed with air can detonate like a bomb. This is a real industrial hazard — NFPA 654 standard exists precisely for this. High SLR + high velocity = extreme danger.
✓ Safe: index <40
△ Warn: 40–70
⚠ Critical: >70
🌡️
Temperature
When blowers and pipes operate at excessive heat, the motor gets damaged, lubricant dries out, and some materials (such as plastic pellets) can melt and stick inside the pipe.
✓ Safe: <60°C
△ Warn: 60–85°C
⚠ Critical: >85°C
💨
Air Leakage
If pipe joints or connections are loose, air escapes the system. This lowers efficiency — more power is consumed and material is not conveyed properly. Dust leaking into the work area is also a health hazard.
✓ Safe: <3%
△ Warn: 3–8%
⚠ Critical: >8%
💨
Velocity Limit
Conveying air must stay within a safe speed range. Too fast causes rapid pipe wear and particle degradation; too slow causes saltation and blockage. Both extremes damage equipment and halt production.
✓ Safe: <22 m/s
△ Warn: 22–28 m/s
⚠ Critical: >28 m/s
🛡️
System Integrity
An overall health score combining pressure, velocity, blower load, and filter condition. A low score means multiple systems are under stress simultaneously — the system is close to a cascading failure.
✓ Healthy: >70/100
△ Warn: 50–70
⚠ Critical: <50
📳
Structural Vibration
Excessive vibration in pipes and supports indicates resonance from unbalanced flow, loose fittings, or mechanical wear. Prolonged vibration causes fatigue cracking in welds and pipe joints.
✓ Safe: <2 mm/s RMS
△ Warn: 2–5 mm/s
⚠ Critical: >5 mm/s
🔒
Rotary Valve / Stuck Valve
The rotary airlock valve controls material feed into the conveying line. If it seizes or jams due to hard particles, moisture ingress, or wear, material feed stops and system pressure fluctuates dangerously.
✓ Normal: >90% open
△ Warn: 50–90%
⚠ Critical: <50% / Stuck

🔴 SECTION 1 — WHAT IS THE EMERGENCY SHUTDOWN SYSTEM (ESD)?
The Emergency Shutdown System (ESD) is an automatic safety layer built into PneumaSIM Pro. When any critical parameter exceeds its safety threshold, the ESD immediately halts all simulation and signals a plant-wide shutdown.
In a real pneumatic conveying plant, an ESD system is a legally required layer of protection under IEC 61511 (SIL-2) and NFPA 654. It is completely separate from the control system — it can shut down the plant even if the PLC or operator fails to respond.
⚠ WHAT HAPPENS DURING ESD?
①Blower motor is de-energized immediately
②Rotary valve feeding stops (no new material)
③All control outputs go to safe state (fail-safe)
④ESD trip code and timestamp are logged
⑤Alarm notification is sent to operator panel
🔁 MANUAL ESD RESET
After an ESD trip, the system can only be restarted after a manual reset. This is intentional — a human must physically review the alarm, correct the fault, and confirm it is safe to restart. In the simulator, use the RESET ESD button in the Safety tab (Tab 6).

🔁 SECTION 3 — ESD RESET PROCEDURE (STEP-BY-STEP)
1
Identify the Trip Cause
Go to Tab 6 — SAFETY. Read the alarm log to find which parameter triggered the ESD (pressure, temperature, load, or explosion index).
2
Correct the Fault
In the Left Panel, reduce air velocity, lower conveying rate, or change material. Remove any active failure modes from the Failure Simulation buttons.
3
Reset the ESD
Click RESET ESD button in Tab 6 Safety panel. The system status will return to green and the simulation resumes normal operation.
4
Verify Safe Parameters
Run a new simulation. Check Tab 2 results: all metrics should be green, pressure below 80 kPa, temperature below 65°C, blower load below 78%.
5
Re-arm the Auto-ESD
Ensure the Auto-ESD checkbox in Tab 6 is still enabled. After a manual reset, the ESD watchdog re-arms automatically and resumes monitoring.
⚠ REAL PLANT NOTE
In real industry, ESD reset also requires physical inspection of the affected equipment, completion of a safety checklist, and authorization by a certified engineer before restart is permitted.

FREE WILL MODE — SAFETY OVERRIDE
SECTION 4 · BYPASS SHUTDOWN LIMITS · EXPERT USE ONLY
📌 WHAT IS FREE WILL MODE?
Free Will Mode is an engineering override that disables all automatic ESD trip actions. When enabled, the simulator will continue to run — even if any parameter exceeds critical thresholds — without triggering an automatic shutdown.
This mode is designed for expert operators and engineers who need to explore extreme operating conditions, study failure cascades, or test the system's behavior at the edge of its physical limits — safely, within the simulator.
🚨 CRITICAL WARNING
When Free Will Mode is ON, the simulator will NOT stop automatically when limits are exceeded. Alarm conditions will still appear in the Safety Tab, but no forced shutdown occurs. The system can simulate catastrophic failure states.
🏭 IN A REAL PLANT
There is NO equivalent to "Free Will Mode" in real industrial plants. Disabling a safety system requires a signed Permit to Work, a dedicated bypass log, a full-time safety observer, and time-limited authorization. Never bypass safety interlocks without formal procedures.
🛠️ HOW TO USE FREE WILL MODE — STEP BY STEP
1
Navigate to Tab 6 — SAFETY
Click the SAFETY tab in the tab bar at the top. Scroll down to the ESD control area in the safety panel.
2
Locate "Safety Override" Toggle
Find the ⚡ FREE WILL MODE card showing the orange SAFETY OVERRIDE toggle switch labeled "Bypass shutdown limits — expert use only".
3
Toggle ON the Override
Click the toggle switch to enable Free Will Mode. The card border turns orange and begins glowing. An orange warning banner appears confirming override is active.
4
Run Simulation Beyond Limits
Now set any extreme values in the Left Panel (high velocity, high flow, failure modes) and run simulation. It will calculate and display results even in critical/unsafe territory.
5
Monitor All Tabs
Check Tab 2 (Results), Tab 3 (Graphs), and Tab 6 (Safety alarms) to observe how the system behaves under extreme conditions. All data is still calculated and displayed.
6
Always Toggle OFF When Done
When your expert analysis is complete, turn the toggle back OFF to restore automatic ESD protection. Never leave Free Will Mode enabled during routine simulation work.

📐 PHYSICS CORRELATIONS USED
Internal Convection:
Laminar (Re<2300): Nu = 3.66 (constant T_wall)
Turbulent: Gnielinski — Nu = (f/8)(Re-1000)Pr / [1+12.7√(f/8)(Pr²/³-1)]

External Natural Convection (pipe in air):
Churchill-Chu: Nu_o = [0.60 + 0.387·Ra^(1/6) / (1+(0.559/Pr)^(9/16))^(8/27)]²
Ra = Gr·Pr, Gr = g·β·ΔT·D³/ν²
Radiation:
Q_rad = ε·σ·π·D·(T_wall⁴ - T_amb⁴) per unit length
ε = 0.85 (oxidised steel), σ = 5.67×10⁻⁸ W/m²K⁴

Nu_p = 2 + 0.6·Re_p^0.5·Pr^(1/3)
Particle outlet T from NTU-effectiveness method

1/U = 1/h_i + R_wall + R_ins + 1/h_o
R_wall = ln(r_o/r_i)/(2π·k_wall) per unit length
